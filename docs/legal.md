# Kak Fit — Legal Notes (India)

> Quick-reference on the rules that shape the coaching platform. Not legal
> advice — get an Indian lawyer to review Terms of Service, waivers, and the
> nutrition/medical features before launch.
>
> _Last updated: 2026-08-10_

---

## 1. Can an influencer coach clients in India? — Yes

**Fitness / personal training is NOT a regulated profession in India.** No law
requires a certificate to coach someone's workouts. Certifications (NASM, ACE,
K11, Sports Sector Skill Council L4/L5, REPS India) are **credibility credentials,
not legal licenses.**

→ Our core model (influencers coaching clients) is legally fine.

## 2. The line — health & nutrition advice DOES need qualifications

Per the **Dec 2023 Department of Consumer Affairs + ASCI guidelines** for health/
wellness influencers:

- Advice on **preventing / treating / curing / managing a medical condition**, or
  on **nutrition / diet**, is expected to come from a **qualified professional**
  (medical degree, or certified nutritionist / dietician / physiotherapist /
  psychologist, per the content).
- Certified experts **must disclose** their credentials when making health claims.
- A **disclaimer** is required: *"not a substitute for professional medical advice,
  diagnosis, or treatment."*
- Enforced by the Dept of Consumer Affairs; penalties under the **Consumer
  Protection Act 2019**.

| What the coach does | Status |
|---|---|
| Workout programming, general fitness coaching | ✅ No certificate required |
| Nutrition / diet plans | ⚠️ Needs a nutritionist/dietician credential + disclosure |
| Medical advice, reading blood reports, prescribing supplements for conditions | 🔴 Needs a medical professional (else = practising medicine illegally) |

## 3. Product rules this forces (build these in)

1. **Capture each coach's credential type at signup; show it on their profile.**
   "Verified" badge for certified coaches — turns a legal requirement into a trust
   feature.
2. **Gate features by credential.** Fitness coach → workouts only. Nutrition
   credential → may publish diet plans. Medical professional → may touch the
   medical/lab section.
3. **Disclaimers + waivers everywhere** — "consult your doctor before starting," an
   injury waiver before a program begins, "not medical advice" on health content.
4. **Medical-report / supplement feature is coach-assist only** — AI summarises
   *for a qualified human*; the human advises. No AI-to-user medical advice, no
   diagnosis. (Now backed by regulation, not just caution.)
5. **No guaranteed-results claims** ("lose 10kg in 30 days") — breaches the
   Consumer Protection Act 2019.

## 4. Other laws on the radar (for later legal review)

- **Consumer Protection Act 2019** — misleading claims / fake reviews / guarantees.
- **Practising medicine without registration** — illegal (diagnosis/treatment).
- **FSSAI** — food & supplement claims.
- **Drugs & Magic Remedies Act** — bans certain health-cure claims.
- **IT Rules 2021** — obligations if we host user content (grievance officer,
  takedown timelines) — relevant to the sharing/social layer.
- **DPDP Act 2023** — consent + protection for personal and health data
  (especially medical reports).
- **App Store / Play policies** — health-data handling and person-to-person
  service rules (also drives the 0% store-tax carve-out for 1:1 coaching).

## 5. Before launch — hand to a lawyer

- Terms of Service + liability waivers
- Coach agreement (credential tiers, what each may offer, indemnity)
- Nutrition & medical features review
- Privacy policy + DPDP consent flows for health data
